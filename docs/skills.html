What can Lobot do?

A personal assistant, two specialists, and the skills they reach for, plus how a day with them actually goes.

The team

An assistant, and two helpers it hands work to.

Each one has a personality drawn from great fictional aides, keeping the best traits and leaving the rest behind. The traits left behind become hard limits. Personality stays in conversation; records stay neutral, and drafts go out in your voice.

Lobot

Your chief of staff
Named for Lando's aide in The Empire Strikes Back: wired into everything, quietly running the machinery. Unlike him, this Lobot talks. It just doesn't waste words.

Calm and direct. Bottom line first, bad news early, and a fix ready (but never applied) when it spots a problem.

Skills

Things the assistant knows how to do.

You can ask for any of them by name, but usually the assistant reaches for the right one on its own.

Capture what happens

Transcript cleanup

Lucy

Cuts the small talk and anything sensitive from a transcript and drops the filler words, leaving one timestamped paragraph per speaker. You approve the cuts, and only the cleaned copy is committed.

"Clean up yesterday's transcript."

Meeting notes

Lucy

Turns a transcript into notes with a summary, action items, and flags, and suggests the decisions, tasks, and risks that came out of it. For a recurring meeting, you choose one Doc per meeting, a running doc with a dated entry each time, newest first, or both. By default that's one Recurring Meeting Notes Doc, named with the project key, with a tab for each kind of meeting.

"Process today's meeting with the client."

Drive sources

Lucy

Maps the project's shared Google Drive against Lullabot's standard layout, watches it for new transcripts and changed documents, and reads them straight from Drive. When a document changes, it lists the facts and records built from the earlier version, so they get rechecked.

"Anything new in Drive?"

Project dossier

Lucy

Keeps everything known about the project and each site in one set of linked files, every fact citing its source, so questions get answered from the record. When a new source contradicts a fact, you see both on the review desk and decide.

"What do we know about how content gets onto the site?"

Journal

Lucy

Logs the work as it happens, closes out each day, and recaps where things stand when you start a new session.

"Wrap up."

Cleanup

Lobot

Once a day, on a schedule if you like, reads back over everything since its last run: catches the journal up, finishes work that stopped halfway, files what never reached a record, clears finished scratch files, and pushes. Anything that needs you waits as a question on your review desk.

"Run the cleanup."
Keep the records

Decision log

Lucy

Records what was decided, why, who drove it, and who approved it, and tracks what is still waiting on approval.

"What's still waiting on approval?"

Questions log

Lucy

Every open question in four kinds: the ones only you can answer, product questions for the client, technical questions for developers or client IT, and quick checks for the records. Each one says who answers it, how much it matters, and when the answer is needed.

"What do you need from me?"

Risk tracker

Lucy

Keeps the project's risks with severity, who confirmed them, the next step, and a dated history of how each one developed. Risks, decisions, and questions can live in one Maintenance Logs Sheet in Drive instead, with each risk's latest update beside it and the full history on its own tab, so it reads well when shared with the client.

"What could go wrong this month?"

Task list

Lucy

Your own one-off actions from meetings and documents, each with a short summary and a due date. Development work goes to tickets, and what you're waiting on goes to the questions log.

"What's on my plate this week?"

Todoist

Lobot

If Todoist is your to-do list, files the tasks you approve there, set up your way, and keeps the Project Brain's copy in step with everything you change there.

"Use Todoist for my tasks."

Work breakdown

Lucy

Lays out what the team will build, site by site and area by area, with goals, scope, open questions, and sources. You mark which items are ready to become tickets.

"Break down the work for the main site."

People

Lucy

A directory of everyone on the project: roles, time zones, and the usernames the assistant needs to look them up in other tools. It never guesses.

"What time zone is the client's design lead in?"

RACI

Lucy

Who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed for each area of work, on both teams, site by site, plus a "who to go to" table and a Roster & RACI Sheet to share with the client.

"Who signs off on content changes?"

Glossary

Lucy

Acronyms, product names, and words that mean something specific on this project, so the assistant reads them correctly, even when a transcript mangles them.

"What does the client mean by 'the portal'?"
Review and report

Tracker pages & review desk

Lobot

Working pages for risks, decisions, RACI, questions, and the kickoff checklist, plus a review desk with a tab for everything that needs you. Questions show the most important first, and you can narrow them by kind or priority to answer them in chunks. Change things there and the assistant files them.

"Show me the dashboard."

Daily board

Lobot

One screen that answers "what do I need to know today?": your last session, unprocessed transcripts, changed Drive files, what's waiting on you, today's tasks, urgent questions, sprint days left and stuck tickets, new risks, and today's meetings. It opens on your first session of the day (the weekly board takes Mondays), and every item links to where you act on it.

"Start the day."

Weekly board

Lobot

The shape of your week on one page: this week's and next week's milestones (with a Slack kickoff message, by discipline, to copy to your team), who's working and who's out both weeks, the sprint, your tasks from your task manager, this week's reports, a recap of last week, and cards counting what's open on the review desk by priority. Opens on the first workday of the week, each item linked to where you act on it; a section that can't connect says so in place.

"What does this week look like?"

Client status report

Lobot

Drafts the weekly client report from the records and last week's report, in Lobot's template or one built for your stakeholders. Every report says what we need from each site's stakeholders, or that nothing is due, and the assistant asks about any meeting this week that has no notes before drafting, so no ask slips through. Once approved, it's filed as a Google Doc.

"Draft this week's status report for the client."

Project journal entry

Lobot

Drafts your weekly entry for Lullabot leadership from the week's journals: status, progress, problems, opportunities. Carries open threads forward and suggests a status color. Can add it to your journal doc marked as a draft until you finalize it.

"Draft my PJE."
Sound like you

PM voice

Lobot

Learns how you write, so the status updates, client email, and tickets it drafts sound like you. It can keep a different style for tickets, email, and team chat.

"Learn my voice from these emails."

PM profile

Lobot

Keeps your voice and preferences on your own computer, so they follow you to every project instead of being relearned. Nothing about a client is saved there. It also learns how comfortable you are with git and the command line, and whether you use GitHub Desktop or the terminal, so steps you do yourself come at your level, and each PM on a project gets their own.

"From now on, keep my reports short."
Tickets and code

Tickets

Pepper

Writes and organizes tickets to a shared standard: clear titles, testable acceptance criteria, sensible labels and links. Drafts a batch as one ticket plan for whichever tracker you use. When the code repository keeps a kenkeep knowledge base, tickets follow the team's conventions from it.

"Turn the ready items into tickets."

Issue Review Board

Lobot

Before any ticket is created, shows you the whole batch on one clean page: epics you can open and close with their tickets nested inside, the tracker's own work type icons, and every field laid out to read. Approve, cancel, or archive a plan there, and filter by status. Leave notes on a plan, a ticket, or one field, and Claude updates the plan from them and learns from your corrections. Nothing is sent until you ask.

"I left notes on the review board."

Jira

Pepper

Explains what Jira's screens are asking for, sets up spaces, boards, and workflows, audits a site into a workbook, and creates a whole backlog from an approved ticket plan. Connecting checks what the Jira tools need on your computer, so a missing piece shows up at setup rather than later.

"Walk me through setting up a board for this space."

GitHub

Pepper

Reads and updates issues, pull requests, and project boards, and reports on sprint status.

"How's the sprint looking?"

ADR

Lobot

Writes up architecture decisions and hands them to the technical lead for review in the code repository, as a draft pull request.

"Write an ADR for the search approach we chose."
Setup and upkeep

Project overview

Lobot

One page that shows how the project is set up and where everything lives: repositories, where tickets go, the project's library of records and folders, your pages, connected tools, and what's still missing, each with a link.

"Is the Jira space set up? Where are the transcripts?"

Guided setup

Lobot

When something can't be done because access is missing, says what and offers to walk you through it, one step at a time on your Mac or Windows computer: a GitHub or Jira token, the hidden .env file, connecting your folders, installing GitHub Desktop. One step at a time or one short list, whichever suits you; no terminal unless you prefer one, and your token never goes in the chat.

"Walk me through connecting GitHub."

Project setup

Lobot

Puts the Project Brain in a private GitHub repository, helps a new PM join an existing one, and when GitHub says no, drafts the request for whoever has to approve it.

"Put this Project Brain on GitHub."

Save

Lobot

Commits each piece of work on its own as it finishes, with a note of what was done and what it was built from, so the Project Brain's history is a paper trail. Pushes to GitHub only when you say so, or on a schedule you set. When your assistant can't push, it tells you how in GitHub Desktop or the terminal, at your level.

"Save and push."

Lobot

Lobot

Checks which version your project has, and tells you when a new one is out: once a day, when the dashboard is refreshed, and in the nightly cleanup. Updates it with a short "what's new" report when you say so, and helps send improvements back.

"Update Lobot."

Lobot health

Lobot

Checks how much of the assistant's working memory Lobot's instructions take up and where a rule is likely to be missed, fixes what belongs to your project with your approval, and offers to report the rest.

"Lobot ignored a rule. Run a health check."

Lobot feedback

Lobot

Notices when a correction would help every project and offers to report it, without any project details, posting only after you approve the exact text.

"Report that to Lobot."

A day with Lobot

Talk to it like a project coordinator.

Open your assistant in the Project Brain folder and say what you need. Lobot knows which skill to use.

Morningstart of session
Good morning. Where are we?

Lobot reads the rules, catches up on the latest journal entry, checks the project's Google Drive for new transcripts and changed documents, and tells you where things stand. On the first workday of the week it opens your weekly board. On other days your first session opens the daily board: a recap of your last session, transcripts still to process, Drive files that changed, what's waiting on you, today's tasks, urgent questions, the sprint, new risks, and today's meetings when your calendar is connected.

After a callmeeting notes
Process today's meeting with the client.

Lucy finds the transcript in Drive (or you drop it in), cuts the small talk and anything sensitive, and writes the meeting notes. Lobot then opens your review desk with the proposed cuts, decisions, risks, questions, and action items, and waits for your approval before filing them.

Middayplanning the work
Turn the ready items into tickets for Jira.

Pepper drafts one ticket plan from the work breakdown, written to Lullabot's ticket standard. You review it, and Lobot creates the tickets in the tracker you chose.

Afternoonreporting
What did we decide about the launch date, and what's still waiting on approval?

Answered from the decision log, with who drove it and who approved it. Then "draft this week's status report for the client" produces a draft in your own voice for you to edit and send.

End of daywrap up
Wrap up.

Lobot closes out the day's journal entry and clears scratch files, so tomorrow's session, or the next PM, starts with an accurate picture. If a session never got its wrap-up, the evening cleanup catches what it missed and pushes the day's work.
