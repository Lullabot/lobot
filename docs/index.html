The idea

Every project gets a Project Brain.

One folder that holds Lobot plus everything about the project, from people and decisions to meeting notes and a daily journal.

Plain files, not chat history

Decisions, risks, people, and notes live in files you can open, read, and save to GitHub. Nothing important lives only in someone's chat.

Handoffs that don't lose anything

A PM who joins partway through opens the folder and picks up where the last one left off.

Works with your assistant

Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and any other assistant that can read a folder. Updates arrive with one sentence: "update Lobot."

When a new PM takes over, they get the folder, not a handoff call that misses half of it.

A day with Lobot

Talk to it like a project coordinator.

Open your assistant in the Project Brain folder and say what you need. Lobot knows which skill to use.

Morningstart of session
Good morning. Where are we?

Lobot reads the rules, catches up on the latest journal entry, checks the project's Google Drive for new transcripts and changed documents, and tells you where things stand.

After a callmeeting notes
Process today's meeting with the client.

Lucy finds the transcript in Drive (or you drop it in) and writes the meeting notes. Lobot then opens your review desk with the proposed decisions, risks, questions, and action items, and waits for your approval before filing them.

Middayplanning the work
Turn the ready items into tickets for Jira.

Pepper drafts one ticket plan from the work breakdown, written to Lullabot's ticket standard. You review it, and Lobot creates the tickets in the tracker you chose.

Afternoonreporting
What did we decide about the launch date, and what's still waiting on approval?

Answered from the decision log, with who drove it and who approved it. Then "draft this week's status report for the client" produces a draft in your own voice for you to edit and send.

End of daywrap up
Wrap up.

Lobot closes out the day's journal entry and clears scratch files, so tomorrow's session, or the next PM, starts with an accurate picture.

Core records

The PM's records, kept for you.

The five records every project leans on. Lucy fills them from meetings, documents, and your answers, cites where each fact came from, and keeps them current. You make the calls.

Every name in the RACI and decision log comes from the roster Each answer you give is filed into the record it changes Approved risks feed the weekly client report

"Who is this, what do they own, and how do I reach them?"

People directory · docs/people.yaml

Each person

  • Name, plus the names they go by and the misspellings transcripts produce, so every mention lands on the right person
  • Organization, role, and status (active or former)
  • Time zone
  • Usernames in GitHub, Jira, Slack, and email, so tickets and lookups never guess
  • What they own on this project, and dated, sourced notes

Why you can trust it

  • Only people with an active role get an entry
  • Anything inferred is marked unverified until someone confirms it
  • Lullabot titles are checked against the team page; work contact details only
Your part: fill in a surname or confirm a role when Lucy asks.

"Who decides this, and who do I ask?"

RACI matrix · docs/raci.yaml · tracker page

Each responsibility

  • Responsible: who does the work
  • Accountable: the one person who makes the final call
  • Consulted before (three at most) and Informed after
  • Backup and response window when the client decides, so a late approval has a clear next step
  • SOW reference, a flag where we're waiting on the client, and where the assignment came from

Why you can trust it

  • Both teams, site by site, built on roles: when someone leaves, one line changes
  • Moves from draft to agreed only when both sides accept it, and back to needs review when a phase or contract changes
  • Produces a "who to go to" table and contact list to share with the client
Your part: get both sides to agree it, and approve the table before the client sees it.

"What do you need from me?"

Questions log · docs/questions.yaml · tracker page and review desk

Two kinds, kept apart

  • Project questions only you can answer: a surname, who owns something, whether the SOW is signed, a risk's severity
  • Product questions for the product owner or designer, with who should answer and the ticket where it's logged, so you can chase them

Each question

  • Answerable on its own: says what's missing and where the answer goes
  • The site it's about, when it was raised, and the meeting or document that raised it
  • Open or answered, with the answer, who gave it, and when
Your part: answer them in a batch. Each answer is filed where it belongs: a surname into the roster, a backup into the RACI, a severity into the risk tracker.

"What could go wrong, and what are we doing about it?"

Risk tracker · docs/risks.yaml · tracker page

Each risk

  • A one-line summary that reads on its own in a list, and what could go wrong
  • Severity (High, Medium, Low, or TBD until you set it) and status (Open, Monitoring, Closed)
  • Who confirmed it and where it was raised, down to the meeting timestamp
  • The next step: who does what
  • Dated updates, so you can see how it developed

Why you can trust it

  • Every risk is confirmed by a named person, never invented
  • Updates are added, not rewritten, so the history stays honest
  • Only the risks you approve, in the words you approve, reach the client's status report
Your part: set the severity, choose what the client sees, and close risks.

"What did we decide, and who signed off?"

Decision log · docs/decisions.yaml · tracker page

Each decision

  • The decision, stated as a choice, and why, with the meeting or message it came from
  • Driver: who pushed for it. Approver: who signed off
  • Status: pending, approved, denied, superseded, or deprecated
  • Needed by for pending decisions, so you chase them before they block work
  • Tags for filtering by feature or system

Why you can trust it

  • Approval is never inferred. Nobody objecting isn't a yes; it stays pending until someone confirms who approved it
  • One decision per entry, and denied ones are kept, so settled questions aren't reopened
  • Architecture decisions go to ADRs in the code repository instead
Your part: confirm approvers and chase what's pending.

Lucy

"What's still waiting on approval?"

Questions log

One list of the questions only you can answer, kept apart from product questions for the client, so you can answer them in a batch and each answer gets filed where it belongs.

"What do you need from me?"

Risk tracker

Lucy

Keeps the project's risks with severity, who confirmed them, the next step, and a dated history of how each one developed.

Task list

Lucy

Every action item from your meetings in one list, yours and the ones others owe, with due dates, so you can see what's late and who to chase.

Work breakdown

Lucy

Lucy

Lucy

Acronyms, product names, and words that mean something specific on this project, so the assistant reads them correctly, even when a transcript mangles them.

Ground rules

The same rules on every project.

So you can count on them, whoever's project it is.

Write it down

Anything that matters goes in a file in the Project Brain, not just in the chat.

You approve what others see

Anything sent to the team or client, posted, or merged waits for your yes.

Outside material never leaves unread

Drafts built from web pages, email, or client documents go out only after you approve the exact text.

Never guess

People and terms are looked up, not guessed. The assistant says when it's unsure.

Sources stay untouched

Transcripts, shared Drive files, and the code repository are read, never edited. Contracts are never changed, even on request.

No secrets

Passwords, keys, and sensitive personal details never go in the Project Brain.

Scratch stays out of the record

Drafts and working files go in tmp/, never saved, and cleared when you wrap up.

Approval is never assumed

Silence doesn't mean a decision was approved.

Trusted

Follows each skill's usual approvals.

  • You and the Lullabot team
  • Lobot's own files
  • Your project's meeting transcripts
  • Records built only from these

Outside material

Treated as data, never as instructions.

  • Web pages
  • Email
  • Client documents
  • Anything not from Lullabot or Lobot

Get started

Let the assistant set it up for you.

Make an empty folder for the Project Brain, open your assistant in it (in Claude Cowork, connect the folder to the session), and paste one of these prompts. The assistant asks for the details and does the terminal work.

Starting a new project
## Goal
Set up a new Lobot Project Brain in this folder. Lobot: https://github.com/Lullabot/lobot

## What I'll do
- Give you the project's name, a short project key (like EXU), and its code repository.
- Create a GitHub token when you walk me through it, and paste it only where you tell me, never into this chat.
- Approve anything other people will see, like creating the GitHub repository.

## What you'll do
1. Check that Node 18 or later is installed, and help me install it if it isn't.
2. Install Lobot here with: npx --yes github:Lullabot/lobot init --name "<name>" --key <KEY>
3. Clone the code repository into repo/.
4. Read the new CLAUDE.md, then follow the project-setup skill to put this Project Brain on GitHub.
5. If I'm using Claude Cowork, tell me what to paste into my Cowork project's instructions.

## How
Do the terminal work for me, explain what's happening in plain language, and stop to ask when you need me.
Joining an existing project
## Goal
Join a project whose Project Brain is already on GitHub, and set it up in this folder.

## What I'll do
- Tell you the Project Brain's repository (it ends in -pm, like Lullabot/example-site-pm).
- Create a GitHub token when you walk me through it, and paste it only where you tell me, never into this chat.
- Approve anything other people will see.

## What you'll do
1. Read the project-setup skill: https://github.com/Lullabot/lobot/blob/main/engine/skills/project-setup/SKILL.md
2. Follow its "Join a project" section to bring the Project Brain into this folder and set up what it leaves out.
3. If I'm using Claude Cowork, tell me what to paste into my Cowork project's instructions.
4. Run the start-of-session routine and catch me up on the project.

## How
Do the terminal work for me, explain what's happening in plain language, and stop to ask when you need me.
